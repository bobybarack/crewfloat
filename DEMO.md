# CrewFloat demonstration script

Target: 2 minutes 50 seconds. Capture the running application. Keep the example data explicitly fictional. Show actual model and PayPal sandbox footage only after those paths have been verified.

## Before recording

Configure and test the real model and sandbox account; confirm trial coverage; prepare judge access instructions. Use a fresh example workspace and an already authenticated sandbox buyer. Hide merchant/model secrets and owner codes. Rehearse the full chain.

## 0:00–0:20: The decision

Show Recovery desk and cash inputs.

“CrewFloat combines PayPal payments and AI analysis to help a contractor recover work blocked by cash timing. In this fictional example, $1,800 is available but $1,000 is needed for payroll and bills. Which payment changes what the crews can do?”

## 0:20–0:45: Compare alternatives

Compare Oak and Birch, then move the usable-amount slider.

“The full Oak deposit makes $4,850 more booked work feasible in this example. That is a scenario, not revenue received. Fees and holds matter, so changing the usable amount changes the plan without changing the ledger.”

Show the minimum-funding explanation and any displaced unapproved jobs briefly.

## 0:45–1:15: Inspect and prepare

Run the **actual model**, showing required tool results, reviewed message and fixed-amount customer link.

“The analyst reads cash, compares deposits and explains the job before recommending an action. It cannot move money or change the agreed deposit. I review the message and prepare the customer payment page.”

If no model is connected, say “This is the deterministic preview.” Such footage alone does not establish AI integration.

## 1:15–1:45: Payment evidence

Show actual sandbox buyer approval and server verification. Cut login dead time without changing transaction order.

“The customer pays through PayPal sandbox. CrewFloat verifies the saved order, amount, currency, job and completed capture. The money still does not enter the budget until I confirm the net amount is usable.”

A simulation must remain visibly labelled and described as such. Replace it before claiming an observed external integration.

## 1:45–2:15: Commit the work

Show the current approved jobs, confirm the **observed** net amount and approve additional feasible work. Use numbers actually on screen; do not assume PayPal's fee is $50.

“The existing jobs stay fixed. With the checked funds, Oak and Elm fit the crew windows and deadlines. Materials are reserved once, and payroll stays protected.”

## 2:15–2:40: Failure case

Show an actual adverse callback if available. Otherwise choose **Test a payment reversal** and narrate it as a simulation.

“A payment can change after work is committed. This labelled reversal test excludes the receipt's funding and pauses new approvals. Commitments stay visible for review; the app does not silently cancel work.”

## 2:40–2:50: Inspect the result

Show decision log and evidence export.

“Every decision is inspectable: inputs, constraints, payment state and analyst evidence. CrewFloat helps the owner recover the week using cash they can actually commit.”

## Claims to avoid

Do not claim earned revenue, profit, saved jobs, bank verification, automatic messaging, autonomous payouts, adoption or guaranteed awards. Report contractor feedback only after it exists. The proof fingerprint is not an external signature.
